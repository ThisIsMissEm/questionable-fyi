# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Bluesky and ATProto users who want to ask and answer questions. They arrive with intent (a specific question for someone) or curiosity (browsing what others are asking). The platform supports open questions, questions directed at specific users, and interview-style Q&A sessions.

## Product Purpose

A Q&A platform built on ATProto where people ask questions, get answers, and discover interesting conversations. "We all have questions, let's get some answers." Success looks like a lively exchange where asking feels low-friction and answering feels worthwhile.

## Brand Personality

Curious, playful, earnest. The tone invites questions without making them feel trivial. Playful enough to make browsing fun, serious enough that answers carry weight. Think Tumblr's personality meets Quora's purpose.

## Anti-references

- Corporate Q&A (Stack Overflow's utilitarian density, stiff tone)
- Generic social media feeds (infinite scroll without character)
- Overly polished SaaS dashboards (sterile, impersonal)
- Formspring/ASKfm chaos (anonymous toxicity, throwaway energy)

## Design Principles

1. **Questions deserve presence.** A question is the core object; give it room to breathe and feel important, not like another item in a feed.
2. **Invite, don't intimidate.** Asking should feel as easy as thinking out loud. Reduce friction and self-consciousness.
3. **Editorial warmth.** The interface reads like a well-set publication, not a software tool. Typography and spacing do the heavy lifting.
4. **Personality over polish.** A little character beats pixel-perfect sterility. The product should feel like it was made by people who enjoy questions.
5. **Federated, not fragmented.** ATProto decentralization is a feature, not a caveat. The experience should feel cohesive regardless of where data lives.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA compliance. Radix UI primitives provide baseline keyboard navigation and screen reader support. Maintain sufficient color contrast in both light and dark themes, especially for the purple/violet primary palette. Support reduced motion preferences.
